# Guide 06 — How long ago is long ago?

**Built** 14 November 2026, without a review stop. Decisions made along the way are listed
at the end. Fact checks are in `notes.md`. Guides 4 and 5 lean on this one.

## The guide in one line

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Single idea | Time goes back much further than we can feel. Each step back is a far bigger stretch than the last |
| Her action | She slides to zoom the timeline out, and back in |
| What she sees | Everything she was just looking at squashes towards "now", and something far older comes in from the far end. Her own life, a pink strip, shrinks until it cannot be seen |
| Misconception to avoid | That long-ago things were all "about the same time ago" (dinosaurs, castles and Grandma as one fuzzy past). That people and giant dinosaurs ever met |

**Taught terms (three):** **timeline**, **zoom out**, **ancient**.

## Opening — birthday candles

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Her own life is a length of time she can feel: one candle for each birthday |
| What she does | Taps a candle to blow it out, taps again to light it |
| What she sees | With each candle out, the child beside the cake gets a year younger and smaller. No candles: a newborn in a blanket. Lighting candles grows her back |
| What she can vary | Any candle, any order, as often as she likes. Tapping the child makes her hop and wave |
| Misconception to avoid | None introduced. This is the "pink bit" she will see shrink on the timeline |
| How we know it works | She blows out and relights candles without being shown, and gets to the baby |

Captions: *"One candle for every birthday. Fewer candles, a younger you."* (10 words) and
*"No candles. This is the day you were born."* (9). After three taps the toy button wiggles.

## Centrepiece — the zooming timeline

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | To see further back you have to zoom out, and every time you do, all you saw before becomes a sliver |
| What she does | Drags the big slider from the star (now) towards the swirl (long ago). Or drags the picture itself: pushing things towards now zooms out. Taps a landmark, or herself |
| What she sees | One line with now at the right. Landmarks slide towards now and shrink; older ones arrive from the left. The stretches already seen show as nested coloured bands, her own life in pink |
| What she can vary | How far, how fast, forwards and back. Stopping between stops. Tapping any landmark for a hop, a noise and its name |
| Misconception to avoid | That the line is stretchy or pretend: at every moment it is an honest ruler (twice as far is twice as long ago). That the left end is "the beginning": mist shows there is more until the last stop |
| How we know it works | She zooms out and back more than once, and notices the pink bit, or her baby self, getting tiny |

The nine stops, each many times older than the last:

| Stop | Picture | Years ago | The line then shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Her as a baby | 5 | 6 years |
| 1 | Dad as a baby (with glasses) | 40 | 46 |
| 2 | The Sydney Harbour Bridge, new | 94 | 110 |
| 3 | A castle | 950 | 1,150 |
| 4 | A pyramid | 4,500 | 5,400 |
| 5 | A campfire and a giant wombat cousin | 65,000 | 78,000 |
| 6 | Some of the first people, as silhouettes | 300,000 | 360,000 |
| 7 | A long-necked dinosaur | 66 million | 80 million |
| 8 | The new, hot Earth | 4.54 billion | 5.4 billion |

Captions, shown when each stop comes onto the line (not before):

0. *"This **timeline** is your life so far. Slide to **zoom out**."* (11)
1. *"Back when Dad was a baby. Your whole life is the little pink bit."* (13)
2. *"The Harbour Bridge was brand new. The oldest people alive were babies."* (12)
3. *"Castles and knights. Too long ago for anyone to remember."* (10)
4. *"The pyramids. They are **ancient**: very, very old."* (8)
5. *"People already lived in Australia, and so did giant wombat cousins."* (11)
6. *"The first people like us, far away in Africa."* (9)
7. *"The last giant dinosaurs. No people anywhere yet."* (8)
8. *"The Earth was brand new. Your pink bit is far too small to see."* (13)

Tapping a landmark shows a short name instead, such as *"Dad as a baby. Yes, really."*

## Moment — the paper roll

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Without zooming, long ago is a very long way. One sheet is her whole life; castles are a whole roll away |
| What she does | Pulls the paper sideways with one finger. A flick sends it flying; pulling the other way winds it back |
| What she sees | Her own pink sheet, then her baby self one sheet back, Dad as a baby at eight sheets, the bridge at about nineteen, then a long run of empty sheets while the roll gets thinner, and a castle at the very end. There, a sign shows five rolls for the pyramids and a truck full for the dinosaurs |
| What she can vary | Speed, direction, how far she bothers to go. Tapping the pictures makes them hop |
| Misconception to avoid | That the empty stretch means nothing happened. The captions keep it about distance only |
| How we know it works | She keeps pulling to find the castle, or says it is a long way |

Captions by distance: *"Each sheet is as long as your whole life. Pull the paper!"* (11) ·
*"Dad was a baby here. Keep pulling!"* (7) · *"The Harbour Bridge was brand new. Castles are much
further."* (10) · *"Still pulling... castles are a long, long way back."* (9) · *"Nearly there. Can
you see the roll getting thin?"* (9) · *"A whole roll to reach castles! Dinosaurs would need a
truck full of rolls."* (14)

## Try it

A real toilet-roll timeline down the hallway: one sheet for her life, eight for Dad as a baby,
about nineteen for the bridge, the whole roll for castles. Then roll it up again.

## Wonder more

- What is the oldest thing you have ever touched?
- What do you think was here, right where you are sitting, long ago?
- How could we find out about something nobody remembers?

## Decisions made without asking

1. **A slider that zooms continuously**, not buttons that jump. She can stop anywhere, and the
   squash towards now is the idea itself.
2. **Now is at the right, long ago at the left**, on both the line and the slider.
3. **Nine stops.** Chosen so each is at least twice as old as the last and each is something
   she can picture. Castles and pyramids are there because she likes castles and they bridge
   the gap between a century and tens of thousands of years.
4. **"Dad was a baby" is set at 40 years.** The notes tell the parent to say whatever is true.
5. **People in Australia are placed at 65,000 years** with a campfire, not drawn figures, and
   the caption says only that people already lived here. No Country, people or story is named,
   and nothing imitates Aboriginal art. The notes give the 50,000-to-65,000 range and point to guide 4.
6. **The first people are plain silhouettes**, so no appearance is invented.
7. **"Giant wombat cousin"** for *Diprotodon*: it is a relative of wombats, not a wombat.
8. **"Giant dinosaurs"**, not "dinosaurs", because birds are dinosaurs.
9. **The timeline is drawn as a plain ruler, not on model paper.** It is a diagram of real
   dates, not a picture of something too small to see, so it does not carry the pretend glasses.
10. **The paper roll goes only as far as castles.** Beyond that the roll would never end, which
    is the reason for zooming; a sign shows what further would take.
11. **A truck, not a mountain**, for the dinosaurs' rolls. About 70,000 rolls really is roughly
    a big truck full, so the picture needs no unlearning.
12. **One supporting moment.** The roll does the one job the zooming line cannot: letting her
    feel a length of time at a fixed scale.
13. **The human figures** (her, the babies) are simple peg people with a yellow face, in no
    particular likeness. The pictures live in `scenes/pics.js`; they would suit `shared/` if
    guides 4 or 5 reuse the timeline.
