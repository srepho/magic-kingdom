# Guide 02 — What is money?

**Built** 14 November 2026, without a review stop (Stephen: "make decisions, I can modify
later"). Decisions made along the way are listed at the end. Fact checks are in `notes.md`.

## The guide in one line

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Single idea | A swap gets stuck when the other person doesn't want what you have. Money that everyone accepts fixes that |
| Her action | She tries to swap food between stalls, then hands out coins and buys instead |
| What she sees | Head shakes and a jam; then the same foods reaching the same people, with coins going round |
| Misconception to avoid | That this is how money was invented. That having more coins is better or worse |

**Taught terms (three):** **swap**, **money**, **price**. "Earn", "save", "spend" and
"share" are used as ordinary words.

## Opening — picnic swap

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | A swap: I give you mine, you give me yours. It works when we each want the other's |
| What she does | Drags one friend's snack across to the other |
| What she sees | The snacks cross over, both munch, hearts in their thought bubbles; then new snacks appear |
| What she can vary | Which snack she drags; dropping it somewhere else (it comes back); tapping a friend makes it hop |
| Misconception to avoid | None introduced. Here every swap works, on purpose |
| How we know it works | She swaps more than once without being shown |

Caption after the first swap: *"A **swap**: I give you mine, and you give me yours."* (10 words)

## Centrepiece — the market

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Swapping jams when wants don't line up. If everyone accepts coins, every trade works |
| What she does | Drags a stall's food to another stall to offer a swap. Taps the coin jar to hand coins out or put them away. Drags a coin to a stall to buy |
| What she sees | Swap mode: whoever would get something unwanted shakes their head and their thought bubble pulses. Coin mode: the coin flies to the seller, the food flies back, the buyer munches |
| What she can vary | Who offers to whom. Coins on and off, as often as she likes. Who buys from whom, including buying something not wanted (a funny "meh" face). Spending all of one stall's coins, then watching it earn them back by selling |
| Misconception to avoid | That money appeared in history this way. That coins are created by buying: the six coins only ever move |
| How we know it works | She tries a swap, meets the jam, finds the jar, and then gets all three fed |

Three stallholders in a ring:

| Who | Has | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wombat | apples | bread |
| Koala | bread | fish |
| Frog | fish | apples |

Nobody wants what the one who wants *their* food has, so all six possible swaps jam.
With coins on, each starts with two. Everything costs one coin. Stalls never run out.
Someone who got what they wanted stays happy for eight seconds, then is hungry again.

Captions:

- After two jams: *"Stuck! A **swap** only works if you both want each other's things."* (12)
- Coins on: *"Now everyone takes coins. Coins are **money**."* (7)
- After three purchases: *"The coins go round and round, and everyone gets fed."* (10)

After two jams the coin jar starts to pulse, so she can find the next step without words.

## Moment — the coin jar

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | You get money by doing something for someone. Then you choose: spend it, save it, or share it. A price is how many coins something costs |
| What she does | Taps apples to pick them for the wombat. Drags each coin she is paid to the ice-cream cart, the jar or the frog. Taps the kite when the jar has enough |
| What she sees | Apple into the basket, coin into her hand. Cart: she gets an ice cream. Jar: the coin is kept and a dot on the kite's price tag turns gold. Frog: the frog buys an ice cream. Four dots gold: the kite wiggles; tap it and it flies |
| What she can vary | How she splits her coins. Taking coins back out of the jar. Tapping the kite too early (its tag pulses to show how many are still needed) |
| Misconception to avoid | That one choice is the right one. Nothing is scored and no choice is praised over another |
| How we know it works | She uses at least two of the three places, or saves up for the kite |

Captions, each shown the first time: earning, price, saving, sharing, and saving up for the kite.
All are 12 words or fewer; only **price** is a taught term.

## Try it

A shop at home with toys and ten buttons for coins: first swap only, then with coins and
dot price tags.

## Wonder more

- If there were no coins, what could we use for money?
- If you had three coins, what would you do with them?
- What is something you cannot buy with money?

## Decisions made without asking

1. **Three stallholders, not four or more.** The smallest ring that jams completely.
2. **Australian animals as the cast** (wombat, koala, frog). They are townsfolk, not a guide
   character: they never explain anything.
3. **The jam is total in the market.** A working swap is shown first at the picnic, so she
   knows what a swap is before meeting one that cannot happen.
4. **Coins come out of a jar when she taps it.** The simplest reversible switch. Earning is
   handled in the coin-jar scene instead.
5. **Buying something unwanted is allowed.** Money buys anything; the buyer just pulls a face.
6. **One supporting moment, not three.** Earning, saving, spending, sharing and price fit in
   one scene. Borrowing, banks and interest are left out.
7. **Prices are shown as coin dots, not numerals**, so she counts pictures rather than reads.
8. **Plain gold coins**, not copies of real Australian coins.
9. **Place to visit: the Royal Australian Mint**, with a note to check opening hours.
