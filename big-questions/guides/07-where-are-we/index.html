Try it: a peppercorn Earth

  1. Find a ball about as big as a soccer ball, and one peppercorn.
  2. Put the ball down outside, on the footpath or in the park. That is the Sun.
  3. Walk away together for 25 big grown-up steps.
  4. Hold up the peppercorn. That is the Earth, and you are standing on it.
  5. Look back at the ball. That is how far away the Sun is, and how big.

You need: a ball about 23 cm across, a peppercorn, and somewhere to walk about 25 metres.

Grown-up notes

Each line is marked Fact well established, Model a deliberate simplification, or Unsure not yet known.

The idea

  • FactEvery place sits inside a bigger one: home in a street, the street in a city, the city in a country, the country on a planet, and the planet among others going around a star.
  • FactSydney is on Australia's east coast, towards the south. It is Australia's biggest city, with about 5.6 million people.
  • FactAll of Australia is south of the equator. It is the sixth-largest country in the world, about 7.7 million square kilometres.
  • FactEarth is a ball about 12,700 km across.
  • FactEight planets go around the Sun. In order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Earth is the third.
  • FactJupiter is about 11 times as wide as Earth, and the Sun about 109 times as wide.
  • FactThe Sun is about 150 million km away. Its light takes about eight minutes to reach us.

What the pictures simplify

  • ModelEven steps. The slider gives each place the same room, but the jumps are wildly different. Street to city is about a hundred times bigger; Earth out to Neptune is about 350,000 Earth-widths.
  • ModelThe planets picture is not to scale. The planets are drawn far too big and far too close together, and they stand still. If Earth were a peppercorn, the Sun would be a soccer ball 25 metres away and Neptune a small marble about 750 metres away. The "Try it" walk shows this.
  • ModelThe planet parade. There the sizes are true compared with each other, including the edge of the Sun. The gaps are not, and real planets are never lined up in a row like that.
  • ModelThe home, street and city are made up. They are a typical Sydney house and street, not a real address. The city picture is a sketch of Sydney's shape: sea to the east, the harbour running west into a river, a bay to the south. The red dot's spot in the city is arbitrary.
  • ModelCoastlines are rough. The lands on the globe are drawn with a few dozen corners each. Small islands are left out.
  • ModelThe Earth has no clouds, no night side and no Moon here. The Moon is left out because, at this size, it would sit more than ten screens away.

Questions she may ask

  • Why don't people fall off the bottom? FactThe Earth pulls everything towards its middle. "Down" means towards the middle of the Earth, wherever you are standing. Space has no top or bottom; maps put north at the top only out of habit.
  • What about Pluto? FactPluto is still there. Since 2006 astronomers have called it a dwarf planet, because it is small (smaller than our Moon) and shares its part of space with many other icy bodies.
  • Is the Sun a planet? FactNo. The Sun is a star: a huge ball of glowing hot gas. The stars at night are suns too, very much further away.
  • What is past the planets? FactOther stars, a very long way off. Our Sun is one of an enormous number of stars in a group called the Milky Way.
  • Does space end? UnsureNobody knows yet.
  • Does anyone live on the other planets? FactNo people do. UnsureWhether anything at all lives anywhere else, nobody knows yet.

Words this guide teaches

City: lots and lots of streets and homes together. Country: a big piece of the world with its own name, like Australia. Planet: a huge ball that goes around the Sun. We live on one.

Go further

Sydney Observatory, on Observatory Hill near the Harbour Bridge. The grounds are free to walk around by day. Going inside and looking through the telescopes is by booked tour, and there are family tours for younger children. Conservation work is under way, so check the Observatory's page for what is running before you go.
