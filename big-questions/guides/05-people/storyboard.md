## The guide in one line

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Single idea | We are related to every living thing. People are one small branch. The first people lived in Africa |
| Her action | She slides back in time and taps pairs of living things |
| What she sees | Family lines growing down and joining; the place two lines meet lighting up |
| Misconception to avoid | "People came from monkeys." A ladder with people at the top. One animal turning into another |

**Taught terms (three):** **ancestor**, **cousins**, **generation**.

## Centrepiece — the family tree of living things

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Any two living things are cousins: go back far enough and their family lines meet |
| What she does | Drags a handle back in time. Taps any two of twelve living things |
| What she sees | Sliding: lines grow down from each living thing and join, the closest relatives first, until all twelve are one line. Tapping two: both lines to the meeting point turn gold and the join pulses; if the tree has not grown that far, it grows there |
| What she can vary | Any of 66 pairs. How far back to slide, and back again. She can predict which pairs meet sooner |
| Misconception to avoid | See the table below |
| How we know it works | She tries a pair she thinks is "not related", such as a person and a mushroom |

How each brief rule is met:

| Rule | How |
| --- | --- |
| Not "from monkeys": cousins | People and chimps are both tips. Their join is a plain dot, drawn as neither. The caption calls them close cousins |
| A tree, not a ladder | All twelve sit on one level line, the same size. A dog is exactly as far from a kangaroo as a person is, and a test checks it |
| People not top, centre or end | The person is 8th of 12 along the row. Nothing marks it out unless she taps it. A test checks the position |
| No morphing | Nothing in this scene changes shape. Ancestors are dots. The generations scene handles change, by births only |
| Mark the model | The tree is drawn on paper with the pretend glasses; only the "today" strip is sky |

The twelve, left to right: gum tree, mushroom, bee, fish, frog, dog, chimp, person, kangaroo,
platypus, lizard, kookaburra.

Captions: *"Slide back in time. Watch the family lines join up."* (10) ·
*"Go back far enough, and every living thing is one family."* (11) ·
for a pair, e.g. *"People and frogs are distant **cousins**. Their lines meet at an **ancestor**."* (12)

## Moment — many babies

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | No animal ever changes into another. Each baby is almost like its parents, and tiny differences add up over many generations |
| What she does | Taps an animal for one baby (one generation). Slides a handle for thousands |
| What she sees | A row of parent → child → grandchild that always look alike. Two cards compare the long-ago one with the one now; after a long slide they look very different |
| What she can vary | One step or many; forwards and back |
| Misconception to avoid | Morphing. The animals in the row never change; only a new baby joins on the right |
| How we know it works | She notices the row looks the same but the two cards do not |

Captions: *"Tap for one baby. Slide for many, many **generations**."* (9) ·
*"A new baby! It looks just like its parent. Almost."* (10) ·
*"Every baby looked like its parents. But tiny changes add up."* (11)

## Moment — where people went

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | The first people lived in Africa, and over a very long time families spread across the world |
| What she does | Slides through time |
| What she sees | Dots for families in Africa only; then dotted routes and new dots reaching Asia, Australia, Europe, the Americas and last New Zealand. A small house marks home |
| What she can vary | Forwards and back; where to pause |
| Misconception to avoid | That people have always been everywhere, or that one group marched the whole way |
| How we know it works | She slides back to see everyone "go home" to Africa |

Captions: *"The first people lived in Africa. Slide to see where families went."* (12) ·
*"Bit by bit, over a very long time, families moved further."* (11) ·
*"Slowly, families walked and sailed to new places. Some reached Australia."* (11) ·
*"Now people live almost everywhere. We all began in Africa."* (10)

## Decisions worth a close look

1. **The twelve tips and their order.** Chosen to be familiar and Australian, and so that the person is not at an end or the middle.
2. **Split dates** (millions of years): chimp 6.5, dog 95, kangaroo 160, platypus 180, lizard–bird 280, mammals–reptiles 320, frog 350, fish 430, insects 700, fungi 1,300, plants 1,500. Only the insect and fungus figures were confirmed against a source while building; the rest are recalled TimeTree-style medians.
3. **The time strip is squeezed** (age to the power 0.4) and the notes say so. A small dinosaur marks 66 million years.
4. **Ancestors are unlabelled dots.** This avoids drawing any ancestor as a living animal, at the cost of saying nothing about what they were like.
5. **"Family line" and "cousins"** are the working metaphor; the notes say a join is a population, not a couple.
6. **The generations animal is invented** and changes smoothly in one direction. Selection is left to the notes.
7. **Arrival dates on the map** (thousand years ago): out of Africa 65, Australia 55, Europe 45, the Americas 17, New Zealand 0.75. The notes give the dispute over Australia.
8. **Other human species are left out.**
9. **Neutral wording about beliefs** is in the notes and nowhere on a child's screen.
