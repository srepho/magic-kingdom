# Guide 03 — Who is in charge?

**Built** 14 November 2026 without a review stop. Decisions are listed at the end.
Fact checks are in `notes.md`.

## The guide in one line

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Single idea | We can't all decide everything, so we choose someone to decide for us, and we can choose again |
| Her action | She asks the town to decide, runs a vote, lets the leader decide, changes minds and votes again |
| What she sees | Shouting and nothing built; then votes stacking up, a leader, and things appearing in town |
| Misconception to avoid | That people vote on every rule. That one candidate is the right one |

**Taught terms (three):** **vote**, **leader**, **government**.

## Opening — hands up

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | A vote is a way for a group to choose: see which one most people want |
| What she does | Taps one of two games, ball or kite |
| What she sees | The friends who want that game put their hands up. If that is most of them, everyone plays it; then a new round starts with new wishes |
| What she can vary | Which game she taps first. Tapping the less-wanted game just shows fewer hands; nothing is wrong, and she can tap the other |
| Misconception to avoid | That the game she taps wins. The friends decide, not her finger |
| How we know it works | She looks at the thought bubbles before tapping, or says "more want the kite" |

Five friends, so there is never a tie. Caption after the first game:
*"Hands up! Most friends chose this one. That is a **vote**."* (10 words)

## Centrepiece — the tiny town

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | A whole town can't decide everything together. So it votes for a person, and that person decides. Later it can vote again |
| What she does | Taps the card showing what the town needs. Taps the ballot box to hold a vote. Taps a townsperson to change their mind |
| What she sees | No leader: all seven shout their own flag and the card shakes; nothing is built. Vote: one by one each drops a vote in the tube of the candidate they like, and the taller stack wins a star. Leader: each need is built straight away, on the leader's side of town and in their colour |
| What she can vary | When to vote. Whose mind to change, and how many. Whether the next vote changes the leader. How much each leader gets built |
| Misconception to avoid | Voting on every rule: the townsfolk are asked once, for a person, and are not asked about each need. That one side is better: both candidates are equally friendly and get the same kinds of things built |
| How we know it works | She changes a mind or two and runs another vote to see what happens |

Two candidates: the wombat (orange flag, builds on the hill side) and the koala (blue flag,
builds by the river). Seven townsfolk start four to three. The town needs, in order and
repeating: playground, bench, tree, bin, fountain, lamp, up to twelve things.

The ballot box pulses after two jams, and again once a leader has made three decisions.

Captions:

- Jam: *"Everyone wants something different. Who decides?"* (6)
- Vote starts: *"Time to **vote**! Everyone picks the one they like."* (9)
- First result: *"The most votes wins. Now the town has a **leader**."* (10)
- First decision: *"The **leader** decides for everyone. Nobody has to shout."* (9)
- Third decision: *"Leaders we choose are a **government**. And we can choose again."* (11)
- A mind changed: *"Someone changed their mind. Vote again and see!"* (8)
- New leader: *"A new leader this time! We can always choose again."* (10)
- Same leader: *"The same leader again. Most people still liked their plan."* (10)

## Moment — three governments

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Small things are looked after close to home, bigger things by bigger governments |
| What she does | Taps any of six everyday things |
| What she sees | The map lights up who looks after it: a dot for the town, New South Wales for the state, or the whole of Australia. One of three meeting houses, small to large, lights too |
| What she can vary | Any order, as often as she likes |
| Misconception to avoid | That there is one government. That this is a sorting test: there is nothing to get wrong |
| How we know it works | She predicts what will light up before she taps |

Bin and park → council. School and hospital → state. Passport and navy ship → all Australia.

## Try it

Leader for the afternoon: two cups, blocks for votes, the winner decides three small things,
then vote again.

## Wonder more

- If you were the leader, what is the first thing you would decide?
- What could you do if the leader decided something you didn't like?
- The most votes wins. How do you think the others feel?

## Decisions made without asking

1. **Two candidates and seven voters**, so a result is always clear and one changed mind can flip it.
2. **She cannot cast anyone's vote.** She can only change what a townsperson thinks; they vote for themselves.
3. **The jam comes first**, as in the money guide: feel the problem, then find the fix.
4. **The leader decides instantly and alone.** Parliament, debate and courts are in the grown-up notes.
5. **Candidates differ only by side of town and colour.** Nothing marks either as better.
6. **The opening is a direct vote among friends**, to teach the word before the town shows voting for a person.
7. **Votes are visible** so she can see the count. Secret ballots are in the notes.
8. **New South Wales and Sydney are used on the map** as the family's state and city. No suburb is shown.
9. **Place to visit: NSW Parliament House**, with a note to check tour times.
