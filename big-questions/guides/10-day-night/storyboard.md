# Guide 10 — Why is there day and night?

**Built** 14 November 2026, without a review stop. Decisions made along the way are listed
at the end. Fact checks are in `notes.md`.

## The guide in one line

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Single idea | Day and night happen because the Earth spins. The Sun stays put and lights one half |
| Her action | She turns the Earth with a finger |
| What she sees | A red dot (Sydney) rolling into the lit half and out of it, while Sydney's sky goes from day to night and back |
| Misconception to avoid | That the Sun goes round the Earth, switches off, or hides. That the Moon makes night |

**Taught terms (three):** **Earth**, **spin**, **sunrise**. "Day", "night", "shadow" and
"sunset" are used as ordinary words.

## The view, fixed for the whole guide

- **The model** looks straight down on the South Pole, so Australia is in view. The Sun is
  off to the left and never moves. From this side the real Earth turns **clockwise**; a grey
  crayon arrow beside the Earth shows that direction.
- **What eyes see** is Sydney looking **north**, because that is where the Sun is from
  Australia. East is on the right, west on the left, so the Sun travels right to left.
- Both are always drawn from the same spin angle, so they cannot disagree.
- The model is on cream paper with the pretend-glasses mark, as in every guide. Sizes and
  distances are not to scale.

## Opening — a ball and a lamp

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | A light that stays still lights one side of a ball. Turn the ball and a spot on it goes from light to dark |
| What she does | Slides a finger across a beach ball to turn it |
| What she sees | The stripes and a star sticker roll round. The side near the lamp stays bright and the far side stays dark; the star crosses from one to the other, then goes round the back |
| What she can vary | Which way and how fast; a flick keeps it rolling for a while; stopping it anywhere |
| Misconception to avoid | That the light moves. The lamp never does |
| How we know it works | She turns the ball until the star has been in the light and in the dark |

Caption, when the star first goes dark: *"The lamp stays still. The ball turns, and the star
goes into the dark."* (14 words)

## Centrepiece — spin the Earth

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | The Earth spins; the Sun does not move. Day is when your part faces the Sun, night is when it has turned away |
| What she does | Drags a finger round the Earth to turn it. Flicks it to let it coast. Touches it to stop it |
| What she sees | Model: the land turns under a dark half that stays put; the red dot crosses into the dark and out again. Sydney's sky: the Sun climbs, crosses and sinks, the sky reddens and darkens, stars and a house light come on; then sunrise on the other side. A little sun is added to a row for each whole turn |
| What she can vary | Speed and direction (backwards is rewinding). Where she stops: Sydney stays in day, or stays in night, for as long as she leaves it. How many days go by |
| Misconception to avoid | A moving or hiding Sun; a Moon that brings night (the half Moon is in the afternoon sky as well as the evening one) |
| How we know it works | She turns it through a night and back to day, and stops it at least once |

Nothing turns unless she turns it. A flick coasts the real way only, slows, and stops.

Captions:

- At the start: *"A pretend picture of the **Earth** from space. Sydney is the red dot."* (13)
- Into the dark half: *"Sydney has turned away from the Sun. Night!"* (8)
- Back into the light: *"Sydney turns back to face the Sun. **Sunrise**!"* (8)
- Each whole turn: *"One whole **spin** is one day and one night."* (9)
- Left still for a few seconds after turning: *"When the Earth stops turning, the day stops changing."* (9)

Until her first touch a faint fingertip slides along the rim to show where to push.

## Moment — the other side of the world

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | It is not night everywhere at once. When Sydney has day, the far side has night |
| What she does | Turns the same Earth, which now has a second, green dot nearly opposite Sydney |
| What she sees | Two round windows, one ringed red and one green, each showing that place's sky. As one brightens the other darkens |
| What she can vary | Speed, direction, where she stops. She can find the moments when both are in twilight |
| Misconception to avoid | That the whole world has day and night together |
| How we know it works | She stops with one window bright and one dark, either way round |

Caption, after a quarter turn: *"When it is day in Sydney, it is night on the other side."* (13)

The green dot is Rio de Janeiro, named only in the grown-up notes.

## Moment — shadows

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | A shadow points away from the Sun. It is long when the Sun is low and short when the Sun is high |
| What she does | Turns a small Earth on a paper card |
| What she sees | The Sun crosses the park sky and the koala's shadow swings from one side to the other, shrinking towards lunch and stretching again. At night there is no shadow |
| What she can vary | Speed, direction, where she stops; tapping the koala makes it hop |
| Misconception to avoid | That she is moving the Sun. Her finger is on the Earth; the Sun only looks as if it moves |
| How we know it works | She swings the shadow from one side to the other |

Caption: *"Shadows point away from the Sun. Long in the morning, short at lunch."* (12)

## Try it

In a dark room: a ball, a torch that stays still, and a sticker for "your house". Turn the
ball. Bonus for a sunny day: chalk round your shadow after breakfast and again after lunch.

## Wonder more

- Who do you think is fast asleep on the other side of the world right now?
- What would it be like if the Earth stopped turning?
- Why can't we feel the Earth spinning?

## Decisions made without asking

1. **View from above the South Pole**, so Australia is on the visible face and the turning is
   clockwise. The alternative, the North Pole view, would hide Australia.
2. **The Sydney picture faces north**, so the Sun moves right to left. Correct for Australia.
3. **She may turn the Earth backwards by hand** (rewinding), but once she lets go it only
   coasts the real way, and a grey arrow marks the real direction.
4. **No tilt and no seasons.** Every day is half light, half dark, with the midday Sun 56
   degrees up, which is right for Sydney at the equinoxes.
5. **The Moon is included on purpose**, fixed a quarter-turn behind the Sun as a half Moon, so
   it is plainly in the daytime sky too and cannot be read as the cause of night.
6. **A row of seven little suns counts whole turns** as days. It is a tally of days going by,
   not a score, and it starts again after a week.
7. **Opening uses a beach ball and a bedside lamp** rather than a globe, to start from things
   in her room; "Try it" is the same thing done for real.
8. **Far-side place: Rio de Janeiro**, 194 degrees of longitude from Sydney, so for a short
   while both can be in twilight together. Not named on the child's screens.
9. **The Sydney scenery** is a plain arched bridge, a gum tree and a red house. No Opera House.
10. **Taught terms: Earth, spin, sunrise.** "Model" was taught in guide 1 and is replaced
    here by "a pretend picture".
11. **Place to visit: Sydney Observatory**, with a note to book and check access.
