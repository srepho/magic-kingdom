# Guide 08 — What is inside me?

**Built** 14 November 2026, without a review stop. Decisions made along the way are listed
at the end. Fact checks are in `notes.md`.

## The guide in one line

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Single idea | Inside you, bones hold you up, a heart pumps blood round, and a brain sends the messages |
| Her action | She slides the outside away to see the insides and the skeleton, and makes the figure run |
| What she sees | Organs, then bones; a heart that beats faster while the figure runs and slower when it rests |
| Misconception to avoid | That blood is ever blue. That the heart is a valentine shape in the middle. That the heart stops when you rest |

**Taught terms (three):** **skeleton**, **lungs**, **pulse**. "Heart", "brain", "bones" and
"blood" are used as ordinary words she already has.

## Opening — hello, body

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Your body is already doing things: beating, rumbling, thinking, moving |
| What she does | Touches any part of a child who is standing and breathing |
| What she sees | Chest: two rings and a thump. Tummy: a rumble and a wobble. Head: a thought bubble with a picture. Hand: a wave. Foot: a kick |
| What she can vary | Which part; the head thinks of a different thing each time |
| Misconception to avoid | None introduced. Nothing inside is shown yet |
| How we know it works | She touches more than one part without being shown |

Captions: chest, *"Thump-thump! Something in there is beating."* (6). After three different
parts, *"What is inside you, doing all that?"* (7), and the toy button starts to wiggle.

## Centrepiece — look inside, and run

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Under the outside are organs, and under those a skeleton. The heart beats all the time, faster when you run and slower again when you rest |
| What she does | Drags a slider from a T-shirt to a bone. Holds a shoe button to make the figure run; lets go to rest |
| What she sees | The outside fades to show brain, lungs, heart, stomach and blood moving; further along the organs fade to show the skeleton. While running the heart squeezes faster, the lungs swell faster, the blood dots move faster and the ribbon of beat dots along the bottom bunches up. After she lets go it all eases back over several seconds |
| What she can vary | How far the layers are lifted (any point between). Running in any layer, including the skeleton. Short taps for short bursts, a long hold for a long run. Watching the recovery |
| Misconception to avoid | Blue blood (all blood is drawn red, darker on the way back). Heart in the dead centre (it is drawn a little to her left). Heart stops at rest (it never stops) |
| How we know it works | She runs the figure, stops, and watches or comments on the beat slowing down |

Layout: picture above; below it a slider (T-shirt … bone) and a shoe button.
From the outside layer the beat still shows as a faint ring on the chest. A thump sounds on
each beat once she is looking inside or the heart is fast.

Captions:

- Organs reached: *"Inside: your heart pumps blood, and your **lungs** breathe air."* (10)
- Skeleton reached: *"Your **skeleton**: all your bones, holding you up."* (8)
- Heart fast: *"Running! Your heart beats faster. That beat is your **pulse**."* (10)
- Heart settled: *"Resting. Your heart slows down again."* (6)

## Moment — bones hold you up

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Bones are what hold you up |
| What she does | Drags the skeleton, seen faintly inside the child, out to the side; drags it back in |
| What she sees | As the skeleton leaves, the body sinks into a wide wobbly puddle with a surprised face. The skeleton stands on its own and waves. Drag it back and she stands up |
| What she can vary | How far out the skeleton is (the body sinks in proportion). Poking the puddle makes it wobble |
| Misconception to avoid | That bones are dead sticks: the notes say they are alive and grow |
| How we know it works | She takes the skeleton out and puts it back more than once |

Caption: *"Your **skeleton** holds you up. Without bones you'd be a wobbly puddle!"* (12)

## Moment — the brain sends messages

| Field | |
| --- | --- |
| The insight | Your brain sends messages to your body, and your body sends messages back |
| What she does | Taps a hand or a foot; taps the head; drags a feather onto a hand or foot |
| What she sees | Tap a part: a spark runs from the brain down a yellow line to it, then it waves or kicks. Tap the head: four sparks, then a star jump. Feather: a spark runs up to the brain, then she giggles |
| What she can vary | Which part; several at once; where the feather tickles |
| Misconception to avoid | That messages only go one way. That the yellow lines are what nerves look like (the notes say they are a diagram) |
| How we know it works | She sends a message down and, with the feather, one up |

Captions: *"Your brain sends a message down, and that part of you moves."* (11) and
*"A message goes up to your brain too: that tickles!"* (10)

## Try it

Feel your heartbeat sitting still, do twenty star jumps, feel it again, then feel it settle.
Then find hard bony parts and soft parts. Needs nothing.

## Wonder more

- What do you think your heart does while you are asleep?
- If you had no bones, how would you get to the kitchen?
- How does your hand know when you want it to move?

## Decisions made without asking

1. **One simple child figure** with a T-shirt and shorts, drawn in code, not a likeness of
   anyone. The clothes fade with the skin, so there is no bare body.
2. **Three layers: outside, organs, skeleton.** Muscles are not a layer; they are mentioned in
   the notes only.
3. **A slider lifts the layers**, the same control as the warmth toy, so any point between
   layers is possible and it reverses.
4. **Hold the shoe to run.** A hold suits a five-year-old better than fast tapping; a quick
   tap still gives a short burst.
5. **Heart rates of 90 resting and 150 running**, inside the Royal Children's Hospital's range
   for her age. No numbers are shown: the rate is shown by the heart, the sound and a ribbon
   of beat dots.
6. **Five organs only** (brain, lungs, heart, stomach, intestines), placed honestly: heart a
   little to her left, left lung smaller, stomach on her left.
7. **Blood is red in both directions**, a darker red on the way back.
8. **A friendly skull** with a small smile, so the skeleton is not frightening.
9. **"The brain is the boss" is shown only for moving on purpose.** The notes say the heart
   starts its own beat and that some reflexes do not wait for the brain.
10. **"Pulse" is the third taught word**, used loosely for the heartbeat; the notes give the
    strict meaning.
11. **A book rather than a place** for going further: no Sydney exhibit could be confirmed.
12. The thought bubble and its pictures come from the shared cast (`shared/cast.js`).
